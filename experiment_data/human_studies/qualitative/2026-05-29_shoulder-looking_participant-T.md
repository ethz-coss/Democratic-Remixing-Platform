# User Study Report — Shoulder-Looking Study

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Date** | 29 May 2025 |
| **Participant** | T |
| **Method** | Shoulder-looking (think-aloud observation) |
| **Platform** | Desktop UI |
| **Problem used** | Distributing costs for shared items in a flat (51 solutions posted, LLM-simulated content) |

---

## 1  Task & Setup

The participant was asked to orient themselves on an existing problem and then contribute to it. The problem — *how to distribute costs for shared items in a flat* — already had 51 solutions posted (all generated by the LLM simulation). The desktop version of the UI was shown.

---

## 2  Observations

### 2.1  Problem Statement

- The participant started by reading the problem statement carefully.
- When reaching the **constraints section**, they remarked *"oh, fairness by usage"*, indicating the constraint felt potentially too demanding or hard to satisfy.

### 2.2  Feed / Side Panel Navigation

- Attention moved to the **side panel** where the **Focus** tab was active.
- They scrolled through the listed ideas and quickly discovered the distinction between the **Focus** and **All Solutions** tabs.
- **Open question raised later:** *"What makes an idea belong to the Focus? How is it selected?"*

### 2.3  Inspecting Individual Solutions

- Clicked on a single idea card and read the detail page.
- Because all solutions were LLM-generated, the content felt **overly technical and poorly formatted**.
- After inspecting **three** detail pages, the participant **lost interest** in the exact content.
- They expressed an urge to **dislike** one solution but then noticed they could only **like** it.
- The **like button on the detail page was not noticed** initially.

### 2.4  Navigation

- The **"Back to Feed"** button worked well and was intuitive.

### 2.5  Graph View

- The participant discovered the **graph view toggle** button and was **visibly impressed** by the overview visualization.
- **Issue:** Jumping/spring animations when switching between Focus and All views felt **disorienting**.
- They explored the graph, identified interesting **clusters**, and clicked into solutions from the graph to investigate.
- Found it **difficult to contribute to or modify existing solutions** because the LLM-generated content was hard to understand.

### 2.6  Creating a New Solution

- Since no existing idea felt appealing, the participant wanted to **create a new solution**.
- The creation flow worked well; the **button was clearly visible**.
- **UI note:** The edit button at the top of the edit panel looked like a title rather than an interactive element — this area may need redesign.

### 2.7  Combining Solutions

- The participant **did not notice or attend to the combine buttons** on solution cards on their own.
- After the observer mentioned the combine feature, they tried it.
- They combined their own idea with another solution but **took nothing from the other one**, effectively duplicating their own idea.
- The merge was created successfully.
- **Concern:** This "empty merge" creates a trivial duplicate. May be an edge case, but worth noting.

### 2.8  Returning to Overview & Further Exploration

- Back on the graph overview, the participant played with the visualization.
- **Issues detected:**
  - Inconsistent node coloring *(noted as already fixed in a later build)*.
  - Jumping graph animations *(noted as already fixed in a later build)*.
- **Unclear mental model:** It was not clear to the participant what defines a cluster (the shared title convention was not obvious).
- The **merging logic** (which cluster a merged solution belongs to) was also unclear.

### 2.9  Ballot Section

- The participant **never looked at the ballot section** during the session.
- The goal of the overall deliberation process had to be explained by the observer.
- Notifications / suggestions to move votes were **not working** in the tested build, so the sliding-focus-window experience was not observed.

---

## 3  Post-Session Interview — Key Takeaways

| Theme | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Content quality** | The LLM-generated content made it difficult to engage on a substantive level (titles, proposal texts, etc.). |
| **Graph overview** | Described as **helpful** for getting an overview of the solution space. |

---

## 4  Improvement Ideas (from Participant & Observer)

### 4.1  Open Questions as First-Class Objects

Currently only *solutions* exist. The participant suggested that **open questions** should be a structural element, not just comments.

Concrete sub-ideas:
- Render the **problem itself as a node** in the graph.
- Let users **add question nodes** that attach to problems or solutions.
- Let question nodes **reference one or two solutions** plus an arbitrary number of open questions, which are then answered by a linked solution.

### 4.2  Clearer Merge / Cluster Rules

The merge rule needs to be explicit and understandable. A proposed model:

1. By default, a **merge node belongs to the cluster of its base parent**.
2. If the merge node receives **enough votes**, it earns the right to define its own title and **encompasses both parent clusters**, integrating all their nodes into a new, larger cluster.
3. **Open questions:** How many votes are needed? Would combining parent clusters produce coherent new clusters, or risk creating confusing groupings?

---

## 5  Action Items

| # | Item | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clarify what determines the "Focus" set — make selection criteria visible to users | High |
| 2 | Make the **like button** on the detail page more prominent | Medium |
| 3 | Redesign the edit-panel header so the edit button is clearly interactive | Medium |
| 4 | Prevent trivial "empty merges" (duplicate-via-merge) | Low |
| 5 | Make cluster membership and titles understandable to users | High |
| 6 | Re-implement sliding focus window & vote-move notifications | High |
| 7 | Explore open-question nodes as a feature | Future |
| 8 | Define and communicate merge-cluster rules clearly | Future |
